# ₱1,000,000 — the plan

**Goal**: ₱1,000,000 of new, attributable money (side income earned + investment
balance built by this plan), tracked on a scoreboard, with the least possible
weekly attention from Jon.

**Honest framing first.** Nobody — agent or human — conjures ₱1M. Money arrives
through exactly three machines, and the plan is to run all three at the level
of babysitting each one deserves:

| Engine | Machine | ₱/effort | Babysitting | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Base | savings rate × guaranteed return | low, certain | ~zero (set once) | ~none |
| B — Rate | hours × specialist rate | high | ~4–8 h/week | needs clearance |
| C — Leverage | asset × sales | spiky, usually ₱0 | agent-heavy | high |

Engine A guarantees the goal on a date. Engine B pulls that date dramatically
closer. Engine C is a funded lottery ticket. The weekly system at the bottom is
what makes the whole thing run without Jon steering.

---

## Constraint zero: employment reality (LSERV, deployed at BSP)

Jon is an **LSERV (service contractor) employee deployed at the BSP**, not a
civil servant. That rewrites the compliance picture:

- **CSC moonlighting rules, agency-head clearance, and SALN do not apply** —
  those govern government officials/employees, and an agency contractor is a
  private employee of the agency. There is no government permission gate.
- **The real gates are two documents:** (1) the **LSERV employment contract**
  — check for exclusivity/moonlighting/non-compete clauses; if one exists,
  written permission from LSERV HR is a far lower bar than a government
  clearance; if none exists, no permission is needed at all; and (2) the
  **confidentiality/NDA undertaking** signed for the BSP deployment — it binds
  regardless of employer.
- **Conflict screen stays by prudence, not law**: while deployed inside the
  BSP, still decline BSP-supervised institutions as clients and anything
  touching what the deployment exposes you to. It protects the deployment and
  reads well to every other client.
- **The handcuffs I designed around don't exist.** No tenure, no GSIS ladder,
  no civil-service career cost to leaving — which makes *changing employers*
  a fully available move, and (see the career lever below) probably the
  single highest-EV action in this whole plan.

## Engine A — Base: automatic, guaranteed, boring (start this week)

Auto-transfer on payday into **Pag-IBIG MP2**: government-guaranteed,
**tax-free dividends**, 7.12% declared for 2025 (7.1% the year before; not
guaranteed forward, but the decade average is ~6.9%), 5-year maturity, ₱500
minimum. When a **Retail Treasury Bond** window opens (H2 2026 is expected;
recent 5-yr coupon was ~6.0%), it's the diversifier for larger lumps.

Time to a ₱1M balance at ~7% compounding, by monthly auto-transfer:

| Monthly | Reaches ₱1M in |
|---|---|
| ₱15,000 | ~4.7 years |
| ₱25,000 | ~3.0 years |
| ₱40,000 | ~2.0 years |
| ₱55,000 | ~1.5 years |

Yes — most of that ₱1M is your own salary routed somewhere it compounds
tax-free instead of evaporating. That is the point: this engine cannot fail,
requires one enrollment + one recurring bank instruction, and zero decisions
afterward. Every peso Engine B earns also lands here.

**Setup (once):** enroll an MP2 account (Virtual Pag-IBIG, ~15 min), set a
recurring payday transfer, done. Scale the monthly amount to whatever your
actual surplus is — the scoreboard tracks reality, not the table.

## Engine B — Rate: cleared specialist consulting (the accelerant)

You are not "a Filipino generalist dev at $15/hr." The sellable profile is:
**central-bank engineer, DevOps + full-stack (this repo is the portfolio),
finishing an MS in Financial Engineering (WQU) + a research thesis**. That
profile sits in the specialist band of the 2026 market: senior PH devs bill
$30–65/hr, AI/ML and finance-adjacent work $50–75/hr. Positioning to sell:
data/backend engineering for fintech-adjacent and quant-flavored products —
*for clients outside BSP's perimeter*.

Math at a deliberately conservative **$45/hr × 8 focused hours/week**
(one client, retainer, not gig-hopping):

- ≈ $1,440/mo ≈ **₱82k/mo** (at ₱57/USD)
- Net of the BIR 8% flat option ≈ ₱75k/mo
- **₱1M net in ~13 months** from this engine alone; combined with Engine A's
  compounding, the scoreboard crosses ₱1M in **~9–11 months**.

Babysitting is minimized by design: **one retainer client** (no pipeline
churn), a fixed weekly deep block that already exists in your Template, and
agent leverage on everything around the billable core (proposals, boilerplate,
docs, scaffolding — Claude's job). The sequence:

1. **Contract check first** (issue filed): re-read the LSERV contract and the
   BSP-deployment NDA. Exclusivity clause → one permission letter to LSERV
   HR; no clause → Engine B is unblocked immediately. Nothing publishes
   before this check is recorded.
2. BIR registration as a professional (mixed-income earner), elect the 8%
   gross option (simplest, no VAT below ₱3M).
3. Profile assets (agent-built, you approve): one-pager, portfolio page
   anchored on Life OS + procurement-verifier-shaped work (sanitized), Upwork/
   Contra profile as channel #2, direct outreach to 10 shortlisted teams as
   channel #1 (direct relationships bill 20–30% above platform rates).
4. Land **one** retainer. Decline the second client. Depth over churn.

## Engine C — Leverage: one asset, agent-built, capped effort

Life OS itself is genuinely a product-shaped artifact — but productizing is a
business activity (permission-gated, CSC MC 32) and most products earn ~₱0.
So Engine C runs under two hard rules: it only starts **after** A is automated
and B's clearance decision has landed, and it never consumes more than ~10% of
plan effort — the build is delegated to agents, Jon only approves direction.
Candidate list (pick at most one, later): a paid template/starter derived from
this stack, a niche tool for the WQU/quant-student market, or written
material from the thesis. Treated strictly as upside, never as the plan.

## The fourth lever — and now probably the biggest: change employers

As a private contractor employee there is no career cost to leaving, and the
gap between a manpower-agency salary and a **remote international full-time
role** (US/EU/SG companies hiring PH-based seniors at ₱150k–₱350k+/mo) is
larger than anything Engine B can bill part-time. One successful job switch
can add **₱1M+/yr of income by itself** — with less total effort than running
a consultancy beside a day job. The same launch-kit assets (positioning,
portfolio, profiles) serve both paths; the difference is applying to jobs vs.
pitching retainers. Run them in parallel and take whichever lands first.

Finishing the **WQU MSFE + thesis** multiplies this lever (quant/fintech
roles), so the plan still *protects* those study blocks — Engine B stays
capped at 8 h/wk precisely so it never eats the thesis.

## The system (what "least babysitting" actually means)

- **Automatic**: payday auto-transfer (bank does it), MP2 compounding
  (Pag-IBIG does it), weekly check-in (a scheduled Claude routine does it).
- **The routine** (Mondays 8pm PHT): a fresh Claude session reads the
  scoreboard issue, updates the running total from any balance/income comments
  you've dropped, recomputes the projected ₱1M date, and posts **one short
  comment only when you owe a decision or action**. Silence means on-track.
- **Your entire job**: read the Monday nudge; occasionally reply with a number
  ("MP2 now 180k", "invoice paid $1,440"); make the ~5 real decisions the plan
  ever asks of you (file clearance, pick the client, etc.). Everything else is
  filed as `ready-for-agent` issues and done by Claude.

## First moves (filed as issues)

1. **Enroll MP2 + set payday auto-transfer** — human, ~30 min, this week.
2. **Check LSERV contract + BSP NDA for side-work clauses** — human, one
   evening; agent drafts the LSERV permission letter if a clause exists.
3. **Build consulting profile assets** — agent, done; publishing blocked on #2.
4. **BIR registration, 8% option** — human, blocked on first paid work.
5. **Remote job-switch track** — agent drafts CV/applications; runs in
   parallel with the consulting track.
